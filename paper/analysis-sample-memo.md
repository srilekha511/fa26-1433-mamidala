## 1. Treated units: subreddits that banned AI

Population: the subreddits in both AIRules crawls (July 2023 and November 2024; Lloyd et al. 2025)
with at least 1,000 subscribers. A subreddit is treated if:

1. its July 2023 rules contain no AI words, and
2. its November 2024 rules contain a rule that bans AI content ("No AI art", "AI-generated content is
   not allowed", "posts made with ChatGPT will be removed"), with no labeling wording (flair, tag,
   label, disclose) in the same rule, and
3. that rule was created after the July 2023 crawl.

**Ban date.** Reddit stores a `created_utc` timestamp for every rule, and AIRules keeps it. The ban
date is the ban rule's timestamp. On the study subreddits from the label design, the same timestamps
match my hand-coded dates exactly for Shrek, sticker, overlord and Simpsons, and within about a week for
digital_art, NikkeMobile and Frozen. Reddit keeps the timestamp when a rule is edited, so a ban written
into an older rule cannot be dated; those 151 subreddits are dropped.

| Step | Subreddits |
|---|---|
| November 2024 rules mention AI, both crawls, at least 1,000 subscribers, ban wording or earlier AI rule | 1,528 |
| Dropped: an AI rule already in July 2023 | 961 |
| Dropped: ban written into a rule created before July 2023 (undatable) | 151 |
| **Dated bans** | **416** |
| With at least 30 posts a month in the year before the ban | 351 |

A random 25 of the 416 ban rules all read as real bans; a few ban one kind of AI content only (AI
songs in r/futurefunk, AI text in r/DarkRomance). Ban dates run evenly from July 2023 to November 2024
(6-38 a month; July 2023 is a partial month). The largest topics are Entertainment (100), Art (37),
Hobby (33), Music (31) and Games (31); 287 are Content Generation communities. Lists:
`data/derived/ban_sample.csv`, code `code/build_ban_sample.py`.

## 2. Outcomes

**Log posts per month** (section 4), from Arctic Shift's precomputed monthly post counts (they match
its aggregate counts to within one post on a random check of 8 subreddits). This is the exit margin of
the model: a ban makes every AI creator either stop using AI or leave, so posts fall only if AI
creators leave and are not replaced. It needs no flair and no detector.

**Share of text posts flagged as AI** (section 5), from the Pangram API. This is the compliance
margin: if creators comply, or AI posters leave, the AI share falls after a ban. It can only be
measured for text, so it covers the bans in subreddits that post mostly text.

Not measured: where banned posters go (spillover), in section 7.

## 3. Comparison subreddits

For each banned subreddit, the 2 subreddits in its AIRules topic with no AI rule in either crawl
(by the AIRules label or AI words in the rules) that are nearest in log July 2023 subscribers, without
replacement: 832 subreddits. In the estimate, each ban is compared with every comparison subreddit of
its topic that also posts at least 30 times a month in the year before that ban (608 enter at least
one stack). Robustness uses only the ban's own 2 matches, and section 4 adds a group matched on growth.

## 4. Posts per month

**Specification.** A stacked event study (Cengiz et al. 2019): one stack per ban, holding the banned
subreddit and its comparison subreddits for 12 months either side of the ban month, with stack-by-
subreddit and stack-by-month fixed effects. Each ban gets equal weight, and no comparison subreddit
is treated inside a stack. Standard errors are clustered by subreddit, since a comparison subreddit
sits in many stacks; with 351 treated units, clustering is reliable. Code: `code/analyze_ban_did.py`;
figure `output/ban_event_study.png`.

**Difference-in-differences against the topic comparison group, six months either side**
(`output/ban_did_estimates.csv`):

| Specification | Bans | Change in posts | 95% CI | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Main: topic comparison group | 351 | +12.5% | +6.0 to +19.3 | <0.001 |
| 12 months either side | 351 | +21.0% | +12.6 to +30.1 | <0.001 |
| Own 2 matched comparison subreddits only | 309 | +9.7% | +4.1 to +15.6 | <0.001 |
| Content Generation communities | 240 | +16.3% | +7.5 to +25.8 | <0.001 |
| Other communities | 111 | +4.4% | −2.9 to +12.3 | 0.24 |
| Drop 5 bans dated by a full rule rewrite | 346 | +12.5% | +6.0 to +19.5 | <0.001 |
| **Placebo: ban moved 12 months earlier** | 254 | **+8.9%** | +2.2 to +16.0 | 0.008 |

**This difference-in-differences is not the effect of the ban.** The pre-trend test rejects (the 11
pre-ban months jointly, p = 0.009), and the placebo is also positive and significant. Banned
subreddits gain about 2% a month on their topic comparison group for the whole year before the ban.
A trend-break model (`output/ban_trend_break.csv`) gives a pre-ban trend of +2.2% a month (p < 0.001),
no jump at the ban (+4.4%, p = 0.20), and a slope change of −2.0% a month after it (p = 0.001).

**Comparison subreddits matched on growth** (`output/ban_matched_estimates.csv`, figure
`output/ban_matched_event_study.png`). For each ban, the 3 comparison subreddits, from any topic, nearest
in mean log posts and in the trend of log posts over a pre-ban window. A comparison subreddit can serve
several bans.

| Matched on | Pre-trend test (p) | DiD, ±6 months | Trend before | Jump at the ban | Slope change after |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Months −12 to −1 | 0.37 | +12.1% (+5.8 to +18.8) | +0.1%/month (0.36) | +13.1% (<0.001) | −0.6%/month (0.07) |
| Months −12 to −7 only | <0.001 | +9.0% (+3.1 to +15.1) | +1.8%/month (<0.001) | +2.9% (0.32) | −1.6%/month (<0.001) |

Matching on the whole pre-ban year flattens the pre-trend by construction, so the second row is the
real test: it matches on months −12 to −7 and leaves months −6 to −1 free. Against those matches,
banned subreddits still pull ahead over the last six months before the ban (about 17% from month −7 to
month −1), peak in the ban month (+6%), and then stop gaining: for the next year they stay within about
6% of their month −1 level.

**Identifying assumption.** The growth-matched trend break reads the post-ban slope change as the
ban's effect if, without a ban, the banned subreddit would have kept the trend it had against its
matches. That fails if moderators write the ban during a burst of activity that would have ended
anyway: the slowdown would then be mean reversion, not the ban.

**Direction of bias.** Bans written at an activity peak make the post-ban slowdown look like a ban
effect, so the −1.6% slope change overstates any negative effect of the ban on posting. The true
effect lies between that and zero. Section 5 adds direct evidence that the slowdown is not AI posters
leaving.

## 5. AI share of text posts

**Sample** (`code/sample_ban_text.py`). The detector reads text, so this test uses the bans in
subreddits that post mostly text.

1. **Text subreddits.** I read the 100 posts each subreddit made before July 2023, before any ban in
   the sample, and call it a text subreddit if at least 25% are text posts of 50 words or more. 83 of
   the 416 banned subreddits pass, and 77 of their 166 comparison subreddits. Because the screen uses only
   pre-ban posts, it does not select on the outcome.
2. **Random days.** For each text subreddit, 10 random days in each of three windows: March-August 2022
   (before ChatGPT, for the false-positive rate), months −6 to −1 before the ban, and months +1 to +6
   after it. Every post on those days is kept, so each post in a window had the same chance of being
   drawn. Days are seeded by subreddit and window, so the sample is fixed before any scoring. This
   gives 33,223 text posts.
3. **What gets scored.** The 56 bans with at least one text comparison subreddit, and at most 60 posts
   per subreddit in each of the pre and post windows (15 in the baseline window), chosen by a random
   rank fixed by the post id. Weights scale the capped posts back up to all text posts. This is 11,858
   posts in 132 subreddits (56 banned, 76 comparison); 11,857 were scored (one 54-word post returns no
   result).

**Measure.** Pangram API, model `pangram-4.1`, pinned. A post counts as AI if Pangram's short
prediction is "AI"; "Mixed" counts as not AI, and both the AI fraction of the text and AI-or-Mixed are
checks. Scoring cost about $1,075 of API credit.

**Specification** (`code/analyze_ban_ai_share.py`). The same stacked design on posts:
flagged = treated × post + stack-by-subreddit + stack-by-window fixed effects, on the pre and post
windows, by least squares with standard errors clustered by subreddit (each subreddit sits in one
stack). On simulated data with a known effect of −2.0 points, the estimator returns −2.1.

**Flagged share by window** (`output/ban_ai_share_windows.csv`, figure `output/ban_ai_share.png`):

| | Before ChatGPT (2022) | 6 months before the ban | 6 months after the ban |
|---|---|---|---|
| Banned subreddits | 0 of 672 | 0.99% (23 of 2,328) | 2.34% (57 of 2,437) |
| Comparison subreddits | 0 of 920 | 2.41% (65 of 2,702) | 3.11% (87 of 2,798) |

No pre-ChatGPT post is flagged (0 of 1,592), so the false-positive rate is below 0.25% (95% upper
bound), well under the 1-3% being measured.

**Difference-in-differences, in percentage points** (`output/ban_ai_share_estimates.csv`):

| Specification | Bans | Change in flagged share | 95% CI | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Main: unweighted** | 56 | **+1.0** | −0.3 to +2.3 | 0.13 |
| Weighted to all text posts | 56 | +1.5 | +0.3 to +2.7 | 0.02 |
| Each ban weighted equally (bans with 10+ posts in every cell) | 37 | +0.8 | −0.9 to +2.4 | 0.36 |
| AI fraction of the text | 56 | +0.9 | −0.5 to +2.3 | 0.23 |
| AI or Mixed | 56 | +0.6 | −1.0 to +2.2 | 0.46 |
| Drop r/coparenting (its comparison subreddit, r/VirtualAssistant, has the most flagged posts) | 55 | +0.6 | −0.6 to +1.7 | 0.32 |

Dropping one subreddit at a time keeps the main estimate between +0.6 and +1.3
(`output/ban_ai_share_leave_one_out.csv`). Resampling whole bans gives a slightly wider interval than
the clustered one (−0.5 to +2.5).

**Correction to my 11/9 agenda.** On the first quarter of the sample, the pooled shares rose less in
banned subreddits (1.3% → 1.9%) than in comparison subreddits (2.0% → 4.6%). On the full sample they
rise more (+1.4 against +0.7 points), in line with the within-ban estimate. The early gap came from a
few comparison subreddits with many flagged posts, and pooled shares compare different subreddits; the
within-ban estimate above is the one to cite.

**Does more AI posting before a ban predict a larger slowdown?** The trend-break model of section 4 on
the 54 text bans that enter it (topic comparison group), with the jump and slope change interacted with
each ban's pre-ban flagged share (`output/ban_ai_share_heterogeneity.csv`). Exit predicts a negative
interaction on the slope change. It is +0.5% a month per point of pre-ban AI share (p = 0.23), and the
interaction on the jump is +0.2% (p = 0.94). The pre-ban shares are small (mean 0.9%) and measured from
at most 60 posts per ban, so this test has little power, but it points the wrong way for exit.

**What this shows.** Detectable AI text does not fall after a ban. The interval rules out any fall
larger than about 0.3-0.5 points relative to comparison subreddits; banned subreddits start at 1.0%,
so a ban that cut more than half of its AI text posts would fall outside it. Either AI posters keep posting
and moderators do not remove them, or they keep posting and the rule is enforced on reports, not
detection.

**Identifying assumption and bias.** The estimate assumes the banned subreddits' AI share would have
risen as much as their comparison subreddits' without a ban. Two things bias it:

- **Catch-up.** Banned subreddits start with a lower AI share (1.0% against 2.4%). If low-AI
  communities catch up as AI use spreads, their share rises faster regardless of the ban, which biases
  the estimate upward and could hide a small reduction.
- **Evasion.** If a ban makes AI posters edit or "humanize" their text so it is not flagged, the
  flagged share falls even when AI use does not. That biases the estimate downward, toward finding
  compliance; finding none makes the no-compliance result stronger.

## 6. What the ban study shows

1. Posting does not fall at a ban. Banned subreddits grow faster than matched subreddits in the months
   before the ban, peak in the ban month and then stop gaining (section 4).
2. The share of text posts flagged as AI keeps rising after a ban, about as fast as in comparison
   subreddits or faster (section 5).
3. Bans with more AI posting beforehand do not slow more (section 5).

Together these favor the **selection** reading from the last version over **exit**: moderators ban AI
during a burst of activity, the burst ends, and AI posting continues. In the model's terms, the ban
does not make AI creators leave or comply, at least for text that Pangram can detect. This covers text
subreddits only; most bans target AI images, which are not scored.

## 7. Remaining risks

- **Posts: activity peaks.** The trend break's identifying assumption (section 4) is the main threat;
  the growth-matched results show the paths diverge in the six months before the ban.
- **AI share: text bans only.** 56 of 351 bans, chosen by how much text a subreddit posts. Most bans in
  the sample target AI images, which the text detector cannot score, so this is not the effect of the
  average ban.
- **AI share: catch-up** (section 5). A check is to compare bans and comparison subreddits with similar
  pre-ban AI shares, or to estimate the change in proportional terms.
- **AI share: thin per-ban samples.** Each subreddit has at most 60 scored posts per window, so per-ban
  estimates are noisy; only the pooled estimate should be read.
- **Ban classification.** The ban wording is matched by a pattern, not read by hand. My read of the 83
  text bans found 8 that ban only images, 4 that ban only AI comments or answers, and 4 that do not ban
  AI content; none has been removed from either sample. Dropping the 4 non-bans, and using the 8
  image-only bans as a placebo, are the next checks.
- **Spillover** (advisor's suggestion on issue #5) is not measured.

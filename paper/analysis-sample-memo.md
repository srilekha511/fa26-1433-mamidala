# Analysis sample and baseline results (Reddit)


This memo defines the Reddit analysis sample, says why each outcome is measured, and reports the
baseline results. It covers Reddit only; Substack and arXiv wait until the Reddit results are done.

## 1. Treated units: subreddits that banned AI

Population: the subreddits in both AIRules crawls (July 2023 and November 2024; Lloyd et al. 2025)
with at least 1,000 subscribers. A subreddit is treated if:

1. its July 2023 rules contain no AI words, and
2. its November 2024 rules contain a rule that bans AI content ("No AI art", "AI-generated content is
   not allowed", "posts made with ChatGPT will be removed"), with no labeling wording (flair, tag,
   label, disclose) in the same rule, and
3. that rule was created after the July 2023 crawl.

**Ban date.** Reddit stores a `created_utc` timestamp for every rule, and AIRules keeps it. The ban
date is the ban rule's timestamp. On the study subreddits from the label design, the same timestamps
match my hand-coded dates exactly for Shrek, sticker, overlord and Simpsons, and within about a week for
digital_art, NikkeMobile and Frozen. Reddit keeps the timestamp when a rule is edited, so a ban written
into an older rule cannot be dated; those 151 subreddits are dropped.

| Step | Subreddits |
|---|---|
| November 2024 rules mention AI, both crawls, at least 1,000 subscribers, ban wording or earlier AI rule | 1,528 |
| Dropped: an AI rule already in July 2023 | 961 |
| Dropped: ban written into a rule created before July 2023 (undatable) | 151 |
| **Dated bans** | **416** |
| With at least 30 posts a month in the year before the ban | 351 |

A random 25 of the 416 ban rules all read as real bans; a few ban one kind of AI content only (AI
songs in r/futurefunk, AI text in r/DarkRomance). Ban dates run evenly from July 2023 to November 2024
(6-38 a month; July 2023 is a partial month). The largest topics are Entertainment (100), Art (37), Hobby (33), Music (31) and Games
(31); 287 are Content Generation communities. Lists: `data/derived/ban_sample.csv`, code
`code/build_ban_sample.py`.

## 2. Outcome

**Log posts per month**, from Arctic Shift's precomputed monthly post counts (they match its
aggregate counts to within one post on a random check of 8 subreddits). This is the exit margin of
the model: a ban makes every AI creator either stop using AI or leave, so posts fall only if AI
creators leave and are not replaced. It needs no flair and no detector.

Not yet measured: the AI share of posts before and after the ban (compliance), and where banned
posters go (spillover). Both are in section 5.

## 3. Comparison subreddits

For each banned subreddit, the 2 subreddits in its AIRules topic with no AI rule in either crawl
(by the AIRules label or AI words in the rules) that are nearest in log July 2023 subscribers, without
replacement: 832 subreddits. In the estimate, each ban is compared with every comparison subreddit of
its topic that also posts at least 30 times a month in the year before that ban (608 enter at least
one stack). Robustness uses only the ban's own 2 matches.

## 4. Specification and results

**Specification.** A stacked event study (Cengiz et al. 2019): one stack per ban, holding the banned
subreddit and its comparison subreddits for 12 months either side of the ban month, with stack-by-
subreddit and stack-by-month fixed effects. Each ban gets equal weight, and no comparison subreddit
is treated inside a stack. Standard errors are clustered by subreddit, since a comparison subreddit
sits in many stacks; with 351 treated units, clustering is reliable. Code: `code/analyze_ban_did.py`;
figure `output/ban_event_study.png`.

**Difference-in-differences, six months either side** (`output/ban_did_estimates.csv`):

| Specification | Bans | Change in posts | 95% CI | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Main: topic comparison group | 351 | +12.5% | +6.0 to +19.3 | <0.001 |
| 12 months either side | 351 | +21.0% | +12.6 to +30.1 | <0.001 |
| Own 2 matched comparison subreddits only | 309 | +9.7% | +4.1 to +15.6 | <0.001 |
| Content Generation communities | 240 | +16.3% | +7.5 to +25.8 | <0.001 |
| Other communities | 111 | +4.4% | −2.9 to +12.3 | 0.24 |
| Drop 5 bans dated by a full rule rewrite | 346 | +12.5% | +6.0 to +19.5 | <0.001 |
| **Placebo: ban moved 12 months earlier** | 254 | **+8.9%** | +2.2 to +16.0 | 0.008 |

**The DiD is not the effect of the ban.** The pre-trend test rejects (the 11 pre-ban months jointly,
p = 0.009), and the placebo is also positive and significant. In the event study, banned subreddits
gain about 2% a month on their comparison group for the whole year before the ban, then stop.

**Trend-break estimate** (12 months either side; `output/ban_trend_break.csv`). It adds the banned
subreddits' own linear trend, a level change at the ban, and a slope change after it:

| Term | Estimate | p |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-ban trend, per month | +2.2% | <0.001 |
| Jump in the ban month | +4.4% | 0.20 |
| Change in the monthly trend after the ban | −2.0% | 0.001 |

**What this shows.** Subreddits ban AI while they are growing faster than similar communities, and
the ban marks the end of that growth: relative posting steps up 4-8% over the month before the ban
and stays at that level for the next year. There is no drop in posts at the ban. Two readings fit:

- **Selection on growth.** Growing communities attract AI posts (karma farming), moderators ban them,
  and growth would have slowed anyway. Then the ban's effect is about zero.
- **Exit.** Part of the growth was AI posting, and the ban stopped it: AI creators leave rather than
  comply, as in answer A3 of the model. Then the −2.0% slope change is the ban's effect.

The two can be told apart with comparison subreddits matched on pre-ban growth (as the label study's
controls were), and with the AI share of posts before the ban: exit predicts a larger slowdown where
AI was a larger share.

## 5. Next steps for the ban study

1. **Match on pre-ban growth.** Re-pick comparison subreddits on the trend in log posts over the year
   before each ban, then rerun. This is the direct test of the selection reading.
2. **AI share before the ban.** Score a frozen random sample of pre-ban posts (open Pangram for text
   subreddits, Community Forensics for images) and test whether the slowdown is larger where AI was a
   larger share.
3. **Compliance.** The same scoring after the ban shows whether AI posts actually fall.
4. **Spillover.** Whether heavy posters in banned subreddits move their posting to subreddits without
   a ban (the advisor's suggestion on issue #5).

Unchanged rule dates for the four original switchers: wallpaper (2024-11-22), Shrek (2023-10-17),
NikkeMobile (2024-04-13) and Frozen (2024-01-05; the same post banned images from the main AI tools).

A comparison subreddit counts only if its label exists and it has no mandate or AI ban over the
switcher's whole ±6-month window, so the group does not change inside the window.

The rule timestamps from section 1 also date the two unclear cases: Avatarthelastairbende's "All AI
art must be tagged" rule was created 2023-07-20 and winxclub's "AI posts must use the AI post flair"
2023-12-13, both after a voluntary period. They would make switchers 5 and 6.

mandate). No estimate is distinguishable from placebo dates.

## 7. Remaining risks

- **Ban study: pre-trends.** The main threat, discussed in section 4.
- **Ban study: classification.** The ban wording is matched by a pattern, not read by hand; a hand
  check of a larger random sample (say 100) is cheap and should come before the paper.
- **Ban study: partial bans.** Some rules ban only AI art or only AI text; those may not move total
  posts. A split by what the rule bans is possible from the rule text.
- **Label study: capture timing.** Arctic Shift captured posts weeks late before mid-2023, so Shrek's
  before-after comparison partly compares capture regimes.
- **Label study: four treated units**, two with a rule change bundled into the mandate.
